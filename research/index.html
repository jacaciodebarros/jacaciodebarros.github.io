Research

Foundations of physics, and what follows from them

Early in my career I became interested in the conceptual problems raised by quantum mechanics and their bearing on physical reality and ontology. That interest still organizes my work, which now runs across the foundations of physics, biophysics, and philosophy.

Research areas

  1. Foundations of quantum mechanics

    Realist interpretations of quantum mechanics and their implications for causality and the structure of spacetime, together with the principles that may define quantum theory — in particular the role played by an ontology of non-individuals.

  2. Mind and brain

    Modeling brain function, and learning in particular, from physically plausible assumptions about underlying and macroscopic phenomena; and the older question of whether quantum physics meets consciousness at all.

  3. Science education

    The fragmentation of epistemological views among physics students and pre-service physics teachers, how it relates to reasoning skills and to instruction, and how these students understand the construction of scientific knowledge.

  4. Foundations of mathematics

    Closely tied to the quantum foundations work: how negative probabilities can describe contextuality, and whether quasi-set theory yields contextual situations in which negative probabilities arise naturally.
